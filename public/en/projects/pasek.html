Pásek: a digital property passport from your iPhone

Pásek is our own app for owners of older houses, cottages and farmsteads that have no drawings at all. They walk through the house with an iPhone; the scan produces a floor plan, area measurements and a 3D model, and everything comes together in a clear property passport that can be handed to an architect or a contractor. We designed and built the entire MVP — the app, the website, support and releases.

StoppedThe MVP did not work out, and we stopped the project. We can build a product in a week — and shut it down just as quickly when it does not work. We are keeping this page as a showcase of the technical solution.

01Why we built Pásek

Anyone buying or repairing an old house usually has neither plans nor dimensions. The usual approaches do not help:

  • Tape measure and paper — slow, inaccurate, and half the measurements get lost.
  • Land surveyor — expensive, with a long waiting time, and unnecessarily detailed for planning a renovation.
  • Old buildings are not square — common apps simplify them, and the measurements then do not add up.

A typical scenario: a cottage owner walks through the house room by room with an iPhone, double-checks the dimensions that matter with a laser measure, pins a photo and a note to a damp wall, and sends the architect a PDF with the floor plan and a drawing for CAD. Pásek is an indicative planning tool; it does not replace an official land survey required by the authorities.

02What we built

The entire product, from the scan on the phone to sales in the App Store. The app works offline, without an account, and data stays on the device.

House scan

An iOS app built on Apple RoomPlan measures walls, doors and windows and assembles the rooms into a complete floor.

AR tracing

On an iPhone without LiDAR, the user traces the floor in augmented reality.

Floor plan and measurements

Areas, perimeters and heights, with accuracy given as a percentage based on what was used to measure them.

Property passport

An overview of buildings and floors with photos, notes, documents, roofs and a site plan.

3D model and AR

A model viewer right in the app and a view of the scanned house in AR.

Exports

A PDF passport with floor plans and photos, DXF for CAD, and a package for backup and re-import.

Land registry

In the test version, the app loads the parcel and address from the public services of ČÚZK (the Czech Office for Surveying, Mapping and Cadastre) based on location.

Website and blog

Product site, terms, policies and a blog in Czech and English at pasek.app.

Support and feedback

A support page right in the app and automatic conversion of beta reports into development tasks.

Releases and sales

Automated tests, builds to TestFlight, and the App Store with a one-time purchase and no subscription.

03Challenges and solutions

Old houses have no right angles

Problem
Scanning technology idealizes walls, and common editors snap corners to 90 degrees. In an old farmstead, the areas would then be wrong.
Solution
The geometry keeps the real angles. We calculate corners from the intersection of the wall axes and assemble rooms into a floor plan without snapping.

Accuracy you can trust

Problem
A scan, a laser, a tape measure and an estimate by eye all give numbers of different accuracy. A single number without context would mislead the user.
Solution
Every measurement carries its source and tolerance, which carry through to areas and perimeters. A manual measurement takes precedence over the scan, and the passport shows where more measuring is needed. Unit tests guard the calculations.

Scanning the whole building, not just a room

Problem
When moving between rooms, the phone lost its orientation and the rooms did not fit together.
Solution
The camera and the AR session stay active for the entire building scan. All rooms are created in a shared space, and the floor plan and 3D model are built directly from them.

A web interface with native capabilities

Problem
The interface is built on web technologies, but scanning, AR and printing to PDF require native iOS.
Solution
Our own thin Swift plugin passes a parametric model from RoomPlan, and all the logic stays in JavaScript. PDFs are produced by native A4 rendering, and files are shared through the system share sheet.

The user interface uses no framework or UI libraries. We generate the 3D viewer, the DXF drawing and the ZIP package directly in JavaScript, so the core features work without a connection. The app and the website are in Czech and English, in both light and dark mode.

Quality is backed by more than 900 checks in unit tests, 111 end-to-end browser tests and visual regression tests of screens in both modes. Tests run automatically before every merge, and each new version is built to TestFlight on its own. Scripts prepare and upload the App Store screenshots and descriptions in both languages.

05Result and shutdown

A week after the first line of code, the first version passed App Store review. The user gets a house scan, a floor plan with accuracy given as a percentage, a 3D model and an export for the architect — no account, no subscription, and data only on their own phone.

As a product, however, the MVP did not work out, and we stopped the project. That is exactly what an MVP is for: validating an idea quickly and cheaply, before months of work go into it.

We know how to see a project through, but also how to recognize when to end it. We offer clients the same approach: better to stop in time than to fund something that does not work.
