Aseko: taking over all software development

Aseko makes control units for swimming pools — dosing stations, water quality and temperature measurement, filtration control and cover drives — under its own brand and for partners. The company focuses on the hardware itself; software development was taken over by a team of two suppliers. As the second supplier alongside Telion, we delivered the frontend of all the apps, their releases to the App Store and Google Play, and automated tests.

01From a small project to all of the software

The collaboration began in 2022 with a small offline app, Pool Cover Controller, which lets a technician configure the drive of a roller pool cover over Bluetooth. The app was also built in a version under a partner’s brand.

Aseko then decided to replace the old infrastructure and apps from its previous supplier with a new cloud platform, ACS, and to hand software development over to an external team. Aseko kept the hardware; the software was taken over by Telion’s team and ours.

  • Indigo Studio — app frontends, App Store and Google Play releases including automation, end-to-end and regression tests.
  • Telion — the rest of the ACS platform: backend, infrastructure and API. Their designer and front-end coder built the shared UI component library with us, for both hybrid and web apps.

02What we delivered

Apps that display and control real hardware around the pool. We took over the original apps and consolidated them onto a single hybrid platform, so the same code runs on the web, on iPhone and on Android.

Aseko Live

Online monitoring of pool parameters, alerts when limits are exceeded, and history. Available as an iOS and Android app and as the aseko.cloud website.

Aseko Remote

Remote control of pool components: operating modes, filter backwash, relays, heating, water top-up.

Pool Control for T&A

A version of the remote control app for Technics and Applications in their colors, published under their own Apple and Google developer accounts.

Back office

An internal app for Aseko and its employees.

Shared UI library

Components, data layer, translations and permissions in one internal package for all the apps.

Releases and testing

Automated builds, test and production environments, TestFlight, Google Play internal testing, end-to-end and regression tests.

03Challenges and solutions

One app on web, iOS and Android

Problem
The apps we inherited took a separate path for each platform. Every change had to be made several times, and releases were laborious.
Solution
Hybrid apps built with Capacitor and Vue on a shared component library. We released Aseko Live from a single codebase as a website and to both the App Store and Google Play.

The same app under another company’s brand and accounts

Problem
An Aseko customer wanted the remote control app under its own brand and in its own App Store and Google Play accounts.
Solution
Colors, icons, names and signing keys are configuration, not separate code. Fixes and new features reach the Aseko version and the customer’s version at the same time.

Who can control what

Problem
Several people access a single unit, and each of them is allowed to do something different. The app must not offer an action that the server will reject.
Solution
Per-unit permissions — 64 separate rules — determine what the app displays and allows the user to change. The rules live in the shared library and apply the same way in every app.

Old versions on phones

Problem
The platform keeps evolving, but some users don’t update their apps. An old version then stops understanding the server.
Solution
Forced updates: the app detects that it is too old and sends the user to the App Store or Google Play before they run into an error.

The apps are built on Vue and a custom shared component library, which we distributed as an internal package. Data comes from the ACS platform via GraphQL and WebSocket, alerts arrive as push notifications, and Sentry tracks errors in production.

Every version went through end-to-end and regression tests. Builds for the test and production environments ran automatically from GitLab via Xcode Cloud to TestFlight and to Google Play internal testing, from where a version could go out to users with one click.

05Outcome

A small offline app grew into a two-year collaboration in which we and Telion handled the software for all of Aseko. Aseko could focus on hardware, and its devices got apps on the web, iOS and Android — under its own brand and under its customers’ brands.
